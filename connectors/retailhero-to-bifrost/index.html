    Template: Retailhero articles and prices to Bifrost tills¶

    The onboarding template Articles and prices → tills (Retailhero → Bifrost) moves articles and prices from Retailhero into the catalogue of the Bifrost tills. The tills keep pulling from the catalogue-api as they do today.

    Retailhero partner account                    VRSky                                       Bifrost
rh.evt.v1.<tenant>.pos_publication.  ─►  nats ─► converter ─► bifrost_catalogue (delta) ─►  catalogue-api
  bifrost-catalogue.batch

    It is marked beta. Retailhero publishes the batches from ValueRetail/Retailhero PR #2746 (Retailhero#2744, not merged when this was written), and the converter and producer are tested against that PR's sample event. Bifrost takes price_incl_vat with vat_code since ValueRetail/Bifrost PR #252. What still stands between it and the tills is under What blocks it.

    Who is master¶

    Retailhero is the master for articles and prices in the Bifrost pilot stores (ValueRetail/Retailhero#2675). Only one master may feed a store. The existing Business Central to Bifrost price path (docs/bc-prices.md in the Bifrost repository) has to be switched off for every store this template feeds. Two masters writing to one catalogue overwrite each other's prices article by article, and the last writer wins with nothing in either system to say so. This template does not check it: the catalogue-api accepts both. Switching the BC path off for a store is a step in the rollout of that store, done before the first message is sent.

    Stores that stay on Business Central keep the BC path and don't use this template.

    What the pipeline does¶

    1. Source, nats. Binds the durable Retailhero's operator created on the tenant's partner account (create_durable: false) and takes one batch at a time (batch_size: 1): a batch is published into the pipeline and acked before the next is fetched. Where the durable starts is Retailhero's setting (all today, ConsumerSpec::to_config in its messaging.rs), not VRSky's. In delta mode a full-kind batch is applied as an upsert and removes nothing. Loading a store from scratch is the full-list variant (#856).
    2. Converter. Takes fact.rows of one batch event and returns {"items": [...], "sequence": <fact.sequence>}.
    3. Destination, bifrost_catalogue, mode delta. One batch is one staged import (begin with the sequence, items, commit; see Bifrost catalogue). full is not used: it replaces the whole catalogue with the message, and a Retailhero batch is not the whole catalogue (a source that can collect a whole list is #841).

    The message the template reads¶

    Retailhero's dispatch (integration::pos_publication::bifrost_events in the Retailhero repository, contract docs/contracts/pos-publication-bifrost-catalogue.md, "Transport to VRsky") runs the Bifrost catalogue profile over one frozen publication and writes each batch as one event:
    Subject rh.evt.v1.<tenant>.pos_publication.bifrost-catalogue.batch
    Stream RH_EVT_V1_POS_PUBLICATION
    Durable vrsky_bifrost-catalogue-batch_<tenant> (made by Retailhero's operator)
    Nats-Msg-Id the event id, bifrost-<publication id>-<batch>

    The body has the envelope of Retailhero's other events and the batch in fact (trimmed from Retailhero's sample, tests/fixtures/pos-publication/bifrost-catalogue/v1/event-batch.json):

    {
  "contractVersion": "pos-publication-events.v1",
  "eventType": "bifrost-catalogue.batch",
  "event": {"id": "bifrost-Pub-1-1", "producer": "pos_publication", "tenantId": "T-A"},
  "stream": {"reference": {"owner": "pos_publication", "kind": "bifrost-catalogue", "id": "Store-1", "tenantId": "T-A"},
             "sequence": "1", "previousEvent": null},
  "recordedAt": "2026-11-11T12:00:00.000000Z",
  "correlationId": "bifrost-Pub-1",
  "fact": {
    "kind": "full",
    "effectiveFrom": "2026-10-31T23:00:00Z",
    "sequence": 1,
    "batch": 1, "batchCount": 3,
    "publicationId": "Pub-1", "targetId": "Target-1", "storeId": "Store-1",
    "profile": "bifrost-catalogue-import", "profileVersion": "1.0",
    "rows": [
      {
        "sourceItemId": "S-A1",
        "importItem": {
          "article_no": "A-100", "gtin": "7000000000015", "name": "Vare A",
          "price_incl_vat": "24.90", "vat_code": "H", "unit": "pcs",
          "category": "Dagligvare", "alcohol": false,
          "effective_from": "2026-10-31T23:00:00Z"
        }
      }
    ]
  }
}

    The template reads fact.rows[].importItem and fact.sequence, nothing else. fact.kind doesn't matter, since every batch goes as a delta.

    A publication can be several events. Retailhero gives each distinct start (effective_from) its own batch, since Bifrost keeps one row per article in an import, and it is to split a large batch across events (not yet, see What blocks it). Each event has the next fact.sequence, and each is a delta of its own. Nothing in VRSky treats one event as the whole list: an article that isn't in a batch stays in the catalogue as it was.

    The fixtures in src/test/fixtures/retailhero are events in this shape: bifrost-batch-full-1.json is Retailhero's sample event as it is, and bifrost-batch-delta.json is the delta batch of the profile's expected-delta.json in the same envelope, numbered 4 as the dispatch would number it after the full package's three batches.

    Retailhero's other events¶

    The partner account also exports product.version, pack.version, gtin.version, price.version and campaign.version (streams RH_EVT_V1_PRODUCT, RH_EVT_V1_PRICING, RH_EVT_V1_CAMPAIGN). They are not for this template. A Bifrost item needs fields from several of them, which a converter can't join; price.version is an authored price, not an approved one; and the VAT rate is only in the frozen POS publication. ADR-0063 in the Retailhero repository says the tills get the frozen publication content, never a price VRSky works out. Each durable is filtered to one subject, so binding the batch durable is enough to keep them out.

    Mapping¶

    For each row of fact.rows, the converter renames each field of importItem into the item, field for field, and drops everything else (sourceItemId, the event envelope, the rest of fact). A field the row does not have stays absent; it does not become null. The fields are exactly those of Retailhero's wire ImportItem (import_item() in its bifrost_catalogue/mod.rs): gtin, category and min_age are left out when empty, the rest are always there.

    Bifrost item field From Notes
    article_no importItem.article_no Retailhero SKU
    gtin importItem.gtin absent if the article has none
    name importItem.name short name in the sender's locale
    price_incl_vat importItem.price_incl_vat the frozen VAT-inclusive price, copied
    vat_code importItem.vat_code H, F, L or N
    unit importItem.unit pcs
    category importItem.category optional
    min_age importItem.min_age optional
    alcohol importItem.alcohol
    effective_from importItem.effective_from UTC start of the price; prices are sent before they apply

    Beside the items, fact.sequence goes to the producer as sequence, and the producer sends it on begin (see Sequence). It is a whole number from 1 that grows by exactly one per batch for each Retailhero tenant and store, across publications. Bifrost refuses the commit with 409 when it already has that number or a higher one, and the producer then drops the delivery instead of retrying it.

    recordedAt is not used. It is the start time of the transaction that wrote the batches, so every batch of one publication has the same value, and Bifrost would refuse all but the first. A batch without fact.sequence (or with null) is not converted: the converter fails it, so it is retried and then lands in the DLQ with the field named. Sent unnumbered it would be applied whatever Bifrost already holds, and a late retry could put an older price back.

    The mapping lives in ui/src/onboarding/templates.ts and is the same file the Go test reads (ui/src/onboarding/testdata/retailhero-to-bifrost.json).

    Prices are strings¶

    Retailhero writes the price as an exact two-decimal string and Bifrost takes decimal strings. The converter uses rename only, never to_number, because to_number goes through a float. The test checks that every price reaches the catalogue-api as the string Retailhero wrote.

    VAT¶

    VAT travels per item. Retailhero's profile gives each row a rate of 25, 15, 12 or 0 percent, and writes it on the wire as the code H, F, L or N, which Bifrost's per-tenant map turns back into the rate (the Norwegian default map). A tenant with another map needs another profile version.

    Retailhero has no VAT rate on its product master yet (ValueRetail/Retailhero#2687). Until it has, the rate is supplied by the sender, which is Retailhero's side of the profile: one rate per product, in the sender configuration. The template therefore has no VAT field, and it does not default, repair or derive a rate. A product without a rate is refused by the profile and never reaches VRSky, so it is missing from the tills rather than sold at a guessed VAT. When Retailhero freezes the rate on the product, the profile reads it from there and nothing changes here.

    Order¶

    Bifrost keeps one sequence per catalogue and refuses a lower one with 409, and the producer drops that delivery. So a batch that reaches Bifrost after the next one is lost: its rows reach the tills only when a later batch or a full import carries the same articles. It is logged as an error (dropped the delivery; its rows reach the tills only if a later import carries them). The guard means an older price never overwrites a newer one, but the batches still have to arrive in order for none to be lost.

    What keeps them in order:

    • Retailhero's relay publishes the batches in fact.sequence order.
    • The source holds one batch at a time (batch_size: 1). It publishes the batch into the pipeline, retrying the same batch if that fails, acks it, and only then fetches the next.
    • The producer runs as one replica and handles one message at a time.

    Where VRSky can still let a later batch through first:

    • A retry. When the converter or the producer fails on batch n (Bifrost answers 5xx, times out or restarts), n waits for its retry (1 s, 5 s, 30 s, 2 min) while n + 1 goes on and commits. n is then dropped with 409.
    • The converter runs as two pods on one durable. Batch n and n + 1 can be converted at the same time and reach the producer in either order. The window is short, because the source forwards n + 1 only after n is stored and acked, but a large batch followed by a small one can lose the race.
    • A crash or a lost connection at the source. The held batch comes back when the durable's AckWait (30 s at Retailhero) runs out, and the next one can be delivered first.
    • A deploy. The source and the producer roll over with a new pod started before the old one stops. For that moment two source pods bind the same durable, and two producer pods run imports, so batch n + 1 can go ahead of n.

    Retailhero setting the durable's maxAckPending to 1 for this event type closes the last two at the source; VRSky never changes a durable it only binds.

    A batch can also go missing without that error. Every time the source hands a held batch back (a stop, a deploy, a lost connection) the server counts a delivery, and after Retailhero's maxDeliver (10) the batch leaves the durable. With one batch held at a time the next one then commits as normal. VRSky logs nothing; the only sign is the MAX_DELIVERIES advisory on Retailhero's server, which Retailhero's contract says an operator must watch.

    Ordered handling per connection in the converter and the producer is not built (plans/backlog.md). Until it is, watch for the error above, and when it shows, send the articles again with a newer batch or load the store with a full import (#856).

    Setting it up¶

    The wizard asks for the Retailhero NATS server, the partner user's NKey seed, the inbox prefix, the durable, the subject filter, and the Bifrost address and API key. The rest is fixed in the template: auth_type: nkey, the API prefix JS.RH.API, the stream RH_EVT_V1_POS_PUBLICATION, create_durable: false and batch_size: 1. For tenant T-A:

    Field Value
    NKey seed the contents of vrsky_t_a.nk
    Inbox prefix _INBOX_vrsky_t_a
    Durable vrsky_bifrost-catalogue-batch_T-A
    Subject filter rh.evt.v1.T-A.pos_publication.bifrost-catalogue.batch

    A server whose certificate is signed by Retailhero's own CA needs that CA on the source (ca_cert), set in the pipeline builder after the wizard: the wizard's fields are one line. See NATS JetStream.

    One store per Retailhero tenant. fact.sequence counts per tenant and store, and Retailhero's durable carries every store of the tenant. The pipeline sends everything to one Bifrost catalogue, so a second store's batches would land in the first store's catalogue, or be refused there with 409 when their number is lower. Until there is a durable (or a route) per store, use this template only for a Retailhero tenant that serves one store.

    Running it locally for a test¶

    For a test on one computer, with Retailhero, Bifrost and VRSky's docker-compose.yml stack all running there. This is for local testing only: production can't reach the catalogue-api this way, and how it will is still open in #843.

    1. Start the stack with the two connectors this template uses, which make up-core leaves out:

      make up-core
docker compose up -d --build nats-consumer bifrost-catalogue-producer

    2. Local addresses are refused unless the operator switch ALLOW_PRIVATE_UPSTREAMS=true is set on management-api (its start check reads base_url and the NATS server), nats-consumer and bifrost-catalogue-producer. docker-compose.yml sets it to true on all three unless the shell sets it to something else; check with docker compose config | grep ALLOW_PRIVATE_UPSTREAMS. It also lets base_url be plain http, so Bifrost needs no proxy in front. Never set it in production.

    3. From inside a container on Docker Desktop, a service on the host is host.docker.internal, not localhost (on Linux, give the services extra_hosts: ["host.docker.internal:host-gateway"] in an override file). Bifrost's base_url is then http://host.docker.internal:<catalogue-api port>.
    4. Retailhero's local broker (messaging-up) publishes no host port, and its certificate names the host nats, which inside VRSky's compose network is VRSky's own NATS. Reaching it from nats-consumer takes a change on Retailhero's side (a published port and a certificate for that name) or a Docker network setup that keeps the two names apart. Neither is written down yet. Put Retailhero's run CA (ca.crt) on the source in the pipeline builder.

    What blocks it¶

    • How VRSky reaches the catalogue-api in production is open (#843).
    • Retailhero's dispatch is not merged (Retailhero PR #2746). Nothing calls it yet (no route or schedule), and a full batch above about 4,000 articles passes the stream's 1 MiB message limit until Retailhero splits it. Also open: whether Bifrost keeps a per-item effective_from later than the import start inside a full import.
    • Removals and full lists. A delta cannot remove an article or withdraw a price. Retailhero's profile refuses delistings and retirements for that reason. The first load and a clean-up need a full import, which needs the whole list in one message (#841) and a named person for the shrink guard.
    • Campaigns and member prices have no place in Bifrost's catalogue yet (ValueRetail/Bifrost#247). The campaign price itself flows as an ordinary price.
    • Order inside VRSky holds only when no deploy runs and every batch goes through on its first attempt (see Order).
    • One store per Retailhero tenant (see Setting it up).

    Tests¶

    • src/cmd/data-converter/retailhero_template_test.go runs the deployed converter config on embedded JetStream against the batch events in src/test/fixtures/retailhero and compares with the payloads there. It also checks that fact.sequence comes out as the same whole number (not recordedAt or stream.sequence) and that an event without it fails.
    • src/cmd/bifrost-catalogue-producer/retailhero_template_test.go sends those payloads through the producer in delta mode to the stubbed catalogue-api and checks that every field, the price strings included, arrives as written with fact.sequence on begin; that a publication cut into three events commits three deltas and keeps the articles of each; and that a batch retried after the next one committed is dropped.
    • ui/src/onboarding/templates.test.ts keeps the template in step with the Go test's config and with the connectors' keys, and checks the partner account settings and batch_size: 1.
